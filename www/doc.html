RIFRMap integrates rpoB rifampicin resistance mutation data from seven sources, described in detail below.

Comprehensive Antibiotic Resistance Database (CARD)

Last updated: {{DATE_CARD}}

The Comprehensive Antibiotic Resistance Database (CARD) [1] is a hand-curated resource covering antimicrobial resistance genes and mutations across bacteria. Each entry is drawn from a peer-reviewed publication and reviewed by expert curators; for most species, resistance variants require direct experimental evidence of increased minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC), while for Mycobacterium tuberculosis — where experimental susceptibility testing is particularly challenging — CARD instead applies a likelihood-based framework linking single-nucleotide polymorphisms to phenotypic resistance across large collections of sequenced genomes. RIFRMap retrieves CARD's current release and restricts it to rpoB-mediated rifampicin resistance records classified under the "single resistance variant" parameter.

Comprehensive Resistance Prediction for Tuberculosis: an International Consortium (CRyPTIC)

Last updated: {{DATE_CRYPTIC}}

The CRyPTIC Consortium [2] assembled a large-scale compendium of M. tuberculosis clinical isolates from 23 countries, pairing whole-genome sequencing with quantitative minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) measurements for 13 anti-tuberculosis drugs, including rifampicin. Mutation–phenotype associations are derived from a genome-wide association study using interval regression on log2(MIC): positive coefficients indicate increased resistance, negative coefficients indicate increased susceptibility, and statistical significance is assessed via the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure to control the false discovery rate. RIFRMap enables interactive exploration of this dataset with filters for effect size, minimum isolate count, and Benjamini-Hochberg corrected p-value.

Drug Resistance Associated Genes Database (DRAGdb)

Last updated: {{DATE_DRAGDB}}

DRAGdb [3] is a manually curated repository of mutational data for drug resistance-associated genes (DRAGs) across major bacterial pathogens, including ESKAPE organisms (Enterococcus faecium, Staphylococcus aureus, Klebsiella pneumoniae, Acinetobacter baumannii, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, and Enterobacter spp.), with a particular focus on Mycobacterium tuberculosis. Each rpoB mutation is annotated with a Protein Variation Effect Analyzer (PROVEAN) score, which predicts functional impact from evolutionary sequence conservation; RIFRMap classifies mutations with a score below −2.5 as deleterious.

PointFinder

Last updated: {{DATE_POINTFINDER}}

PointFinder [4] is a curated database that compiles mutations and genetic variations associated with antimicrobial resistance in key bacterial pathogens, including Campylobacter, Enterococcus faecalis, Enterococcus faecium, Escherichia coli, Helicobacter pylori, Klebsiella, Mycobacterium tuberculosis, Neisseria gonorrhoeae, Plasmodium falciparum, Salmonella, and Staphylococcus aureus. It is part of the broader ResFinder [5] framework, which combines acquired resistance genes, chromosomal mutations, and analysis tools to support antimicrobial resistance detection. In this application, the dataset is filtered to include only mutations in the rpoB gene associated with rifampicin resistance.

Reference Gene Catalog

Last updated: {{DATE_REFGENES}}

Reference Gene Catalog [6] (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pathogens/refgene/) is a curated resource managed by the NCBI Pathogen Detection project, developed as part of broader efforts to address the global threat of antimicrobial resistance (AMR). It provides access to a standardized set of antimicrobial resistance genes and proteins, stored in the Bacterial Antimicrobial Resistance Reference Gene Database (BioProject PRJNA313047). This resource is integrated with other NCBI tools (e.g., AMRFinderPlus and the Isolates Browser) to facilitate comprehensive analysis of genomic data. Within RIFRMap, the dataset is restricted to mutations in the rpoB gene associated with the rifampin subclass.

RifReport

Last updated: {{DATE_RIFREPORT}}

The curated dataset was originally assembled by Bolourchi et al. through a systematic literature survey to compile a comprehensive catalog of reported rifampicin resistance-associated rpoB mutations [7]. Studies were identified through Google Scholar using the following key words: “rifampicin resistance,” “RNA polymerase subunit beta,” “point mutation,” and “amino acid substitution.” The dataset is maintained as a continuously updated repository at https://github.com/JanEngelstaedter/ResistanceSurveys, from which RIFRMap retrieves the most current version. Users can filter the data based on the number of species containing a mutation and the number of publications reporting it.

Yang-RIF (2023)

Last updated: {{DATE_YANG}}

The Yang-RIF dataset originates from a study by Yang and colleagues [8], who used multiplex automated genome engineering to generate a saturated pool of all 760 variants across the complete rifampicin-binding site of E. coli RNA polymerase (positions 510–537 and 563–572, E. coli numbering). Drug-specific effects were quantified by normalizing mutant abundances after rifampicin treatment against abundances under untreated growth conditions, yielding a log2 fold-change (log2FC) for each substitution. Within RIFRMap, users can filter the dataset using a log2FC threshold to retain only mutations showing significant rifampicin-driven enrichment.
