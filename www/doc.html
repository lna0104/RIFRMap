Comprehensive Antibiotic Resistance Database (CARD)

The Comprehensive Antibiotic Resistance Database (CARD) [1] is a hand-curated resource developed to cover the full spectrum of antimicrobial resistance genes (ARGs). To be included, an AMR determinant must be reported in a peer-reviewed publication, have a corresponding sequence deposited in GenBank, and show clear experimental evidence of increased minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) compared with control strains. For Mycobacterium tuberculosis, additional curated mutation data were integrated from several major resistance catalogs, including CRyPTIC, ReSeqTB, and the WHO 2023 mutation catalog.

Comprehensive Resistance Prediction for Tuberculosis: an International Consortium (CRyPTIC)

The CRyPTIC Consortium [2] generated a large-scale dataset by measuring quantitative MICs for 13 anti-tuberculosis drugs—including rifampicin— in over 15,000 globally collected M. tuberculosis isolates. Using multivariable linear mixed models alongside whole-genome sequencing, the study identified variants associated with increased MICs (resistance) and decreased MICs (hypersusceptibility). RIFRMap enables interactive exploration of this dataset with filters for effect size, variant count and adjusted p-value.

Drug Resistance Associated Genes Database (DRAGdb)

DRAGdb [3] is a manually curated repository of mutational data for drug resistance-associated genes (DRAGs) across major bacterial pathogens, including ESKAPE organisms (Enterococcus faecium, Staphylococcus aureus, Klebsiella pneumoniae, Acinetobacter baumannii, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, and Enterobacter spp.), with a particular focus on Mycobacterium tuberculosis. The database incorporates PROVEAN scores to predict the functional impact of gene mutations.

Yang-RIF (2023)

The Yang-RIF dataset is based on a study by Yang and colleagues, who performed saturation mutagenesis of the rifampicin-binding site in E. coli RNA polymerase [4]. They constructed single-residue rpoB mutants spanning positions 510–537 and 563–572 to systematically assess resistance phenotypes. Within RIFRMap, users can filter the dataset based on mutation frequency changes under rifampicin selection, using thresholds on log2 fold change (log2FC) to identify enriched variants.

PointFinder

PointFinder [5] is a curated database that compiles mutations and genetic variations associated with antimicrobial resistance in key bacterial pathogens, including Campylobacter, Enterococcus faecalis, Enterococcus faecium, Escherichia coli, Helicobacter pylori, Klebsiella, Mycobacterium tuberculosis, Neisseria gonorrhoeae, Plasmodium falciparum, Salmonella, and Staphylococcus aureus. It is part of the broader ResFinder [6] framework, which combines acquired resistance genes, chromosomal mutations, and analysis tools to support antimicrobial resistance detection. In this application, the dataset is filtered to include only mutations in the rpoB gene associated with rifampicin resistance.

Reference Gene Catalog

Reference Gene Catalog (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pathogens/refgene/) is a curated resource managed by the NCBI Pathogen Detection project, developed as part of broader efforts to address the global threat of antimicrobial resistance (AMR). It provides access to a standardized set of antimicrobial resistance genes and proteins, stored in the Bacterial Antimicrobial Resistance Reference Gene Database (BioProject PRJNA313047). This resource is integrated with other NCBI tools (e.g., AMRFinderPlus and the Isolates Browser) to facilitate comprehensive analysis of genomic data. Within RIFRMap, the dataset is restricted to mutations in the rpoB gene associated with the rifampin subclass.

Curated Data

This curated dataset was developed by the ALJElab through a comprehensive literature review of rifampicin resistance mutations in rpoB [7]. Users can filter the data based on the number of species containing a mutation and the number of publications reporting it.
