An interactive platform for integrating and visualizing rifampicin resistance mutations across bacteria

About Rifampicin Resistance

Rifampicin (RIF) is a frontline antibiotic widely used in the treatment of tuberculosis and other bacterial infections. Resistance most commonly arises through mutations in the rpoB gene, which encodes the beta subunit of RNA polymerase.

These mutations alter the drug-binding site, reducing antibiotic effectiveness and contributing to the global burden of antimicrobial resistance.

Almost all resistance-associated mutations are concentrated within four regions of rpoB, known as clusters I, II, III, and N. These are mutational hotspots where resistance repeatedly evolves across diverse bacterial taxa.

RIFRMap enables systematic exploration of these mutations across multiple curated datasets (CARD, CRyPTIC, DRAGdb, Yang, and curated data), allowing comparative analysis and deeper insights into resistance patterns.
